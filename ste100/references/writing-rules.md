# ASD-STE100 Issue 9 — Part 1 writing rules

The 65 rules and 8 general recommendations, condensed. Rule numbers are the
official ones. Cite them when you report a correction.

---

## Section 1 — Words

**1.1 Use words that are approved in the dictionary, technical nouns, or technical verbs.**
Everything else is not permitted. A technical noun refers to a specified concept
in a subject field. A technical verb refers to a specified process in a subject
field.

**1.2 Use an approved word only as its specified part of speech.**
"Test" is an approved noun, not a verb. Do not write "Test the system for leaks";
write "Do the leak test of the system." "Dim" is an approved adjective, not a
verb: "Set the lights to the dim position."
When the approved alternative has a different part of speech, change the
sentence construction: "Make sure that the valve is operable" → "Make sure that
the valve can operate."

**1.3 Use an approved word only with its approved meaning.**
"Follow" means "come after, go after" only. "Follow the safety instructions" is
wrong; write "Obey the safety instructions."

**1.4 Use only the approved forms of verbs and adjectives.**
The dictionary lists each verb's forms (REMOVE, REMOVES, REMOVED, REMOVED) and
each adjective's comparative and superlative (SLOW, SLOWER, SLOWEST). Adjectives
that form comparatives with "more" and "most" are not listed, because "more" and
"most" are approved.

**1.5 You can use words that fit a technical noun category.** The 22 categories:

1. Official parts information — bolt, cable, filter, switch, transceiver
2. Vehicles or machines, and locations on them — aircraft, cabin, deck, truck
3. Tools and support equipment — blade, clamp, display, drill, gauge, stand
4. Materials, consumables, unwanted material — acid, debris, dust, fuel, oil
5. Facilities, infrastructure, logistic procedures — airport, hangar, shop, store
6. Systems, components and circuits — exhaust, hardware, inlet, pump, standby mode
7. Mathematical, scientific, engineering terms and formulas — average, defect,
   failure, load, performance, pressure, process, ratio, signal, temperature
8. Navigation and geographic terms — altitude, axis, coordinates, deviation, north
9. Numbers, units of measurement and time — 92, hour (h), kilogram (kg), first (1st)
10. Quoted text — EXIT sign, ON position, NEXT button, FAULT legend
11. Professional roles, individuals, groups, organizations, geopolitical entities —
    captain, crew, manufacturer, operator, FAA, United States of America
12. Parts of the body — blood, ear, hand, lung, skin
13. Common personal effects, food, beverages — clothing, coffee, footwear
14. Medical terms — allergy, dizziness, medication, pulse, virus
15. Official documents, parts of documentation, standards, guidelines — chapter,
    checklist, diagram, figure, note, paragraph, section, table, warning
16. Environmental and operational conditions — humidity, ice, rain, wind
17. Colors — black, gray, green, red, white. (Colors are adjectives, but STE
    treats them as technical nouns. Comparative and superlative forms are not
    permitted: no "blacker", no "the reddest".)
18. Damage terms — corrosion, crack, dent, distortion, scratch, stain
19. Computer science, information and communication technology — AI, artificial
    intelligence, authentication, backup, backup file, chatbot, content,
    cybersecurity, database, deep learning, e-mail, field, file, firewall, HTML,
    icon, interface, internet, laptop, large language model, machine learning,
    memory, metadata, network, operating system, plug-in, prompt engineering,
    screen, search engine, status bar, store, token, tuning, update, XML
20. Civil and military operations — customer, deployment, end user, lifecycle,
    mission, obsolescence, operator, OEM
21. Law and regulations — compliance, contract, jurisdiction, scope, waiver
22. Animals, plants, other life forms — bacteria, bird, fern, insect

**1.6 Use a word that is not approved in the dictionary only when it is a
technical noun or part of a technical noun.**
"Base" is not approved as a general word, but "The base of the triangle is 5 cm"
is correct (category 7). "Backup" is not approved, but "Do the backup of the
computer" is correct (category 19).

**1.7 Do not use technical nouns as verbs.**
"Oil the steel surfaces" → "Apply oil to the steel surfaces."
"If you think it will snow" → "If you think that snow will fall."

**1.8 Use technical nouns that are approved in your company, industry, or subject field.**

**1.9 Select a technical noun that is short (not more than three words) and easy to understand.**

**1.10 Do not use regional, slang, or jargon words as technical nouns.**
"Do not brick the router" → "Do not set the router to OFF."
"Remove your gear" → "Remove your tools and equipment."

**1.11 Do not use different technical nouns for the same item.**
Pick one term for a component and use it every time.

**1.12 You can use verbs that fit a technical verb category.** The 4 categories:

1. Manufacturing processes — drill, grind, ream, weld, anneal, polish, cast
2. Computer processes and applications —
   a) input/output: click, digitize, enter, press, print, swipe, tap, type
   b) user interface: clear, close, copy, cut, delete, disable, drag, enable,
      encrypt, erase, filter, highlight, maximize, minimize, navigate, open,
      paste, save, scroll, sort, store, validate, zoom
   c) system operations: abort, boot, debug, download, format, install, load,
      manage, process, reboot, update, upgrade, upload
3. Instructions and information for subject fields — engineering (convert,
   detect, modulate), medical, civil and military, navigation, automotive,
   energy
4. Law and regulations — acknowledge, comply with, conform to, describe,
   enforce, explain, inform, notify, regulate, sign, supersede, waive

If an approved dictionary verb gives the same instruction, use the approved verb
instead. "If you detect broken wires" → "If you find broken wires." But "The
security scanner detects metal objects" is correct.

**1.13 Do not use technical verbs as nouns.**
"Give the hole 0.20-inch ream" → "Ream the hole to a 0.20-inch dimension."
The past participle as an adjective is permitted: "Lubricate the reamed hole."

**1.14 Use American English spelling.**
color not colour, fiber not fibre, analyze not analyse.

## Section 2 — Multi-word nouns

**2.1 Write multi-word nouns of no more than three words.**
"Runway light connection resistance calibration" (5) →
"Calibration of the resistance of the runway light connection."
Use prepositions (of, on, in, for) to break long strings apart.

**2.2 When a technical noun has more than three words, write it in full, then
either give a shorter form or use hyphens.**
Method 1: write it in full at first use, define a short form, then use the short
form. "The Main Fuel Metering Unit (MFMU) ... The MFMU is installed ..."
Method 2: hyphenate the words that act as one unit. Hyphenated groups count as
one word. "Move the main-gear-door retraction-winch handle" (3 words).
Do not hyphenate more than three words into one group.

## Section 3 — Verbs

**3.1 Use only the verb forms given in the dictionary.**

**3.2 Use only these forms and tenses:** infinitive, imperative, simple present,
simple past, simple future, past participle (as an adjective).
Not permitted: present perfect (has adjusted), past perfect (had adjusted),
present/past progressive (is adjusting, was adjusting), and all other complex
constructions.

**3.3 Use the past participle form as an adjective** — before a noun, or after
a form of "be", "become", or "stay". "Examine all parts of the disassembled
unit." "When the unit is fully disassembled, clean all the parts."

**3.4 Do not use auxiliary verbs to make complex verb constructions.**
"The operator has adjusted the linkage" → "The operator adjusted the linkage."
"The volume control can be adjusted" → "You can adjust the volume control."
"The temperature must be adjusted" → "Adjust the temperature."

**3.5 Use the "-ing" form only as a technical noun or as a modifier in a
technical noun.**
"When you are doing this procedure" → "When you do this procedure."
Permitted as technical nouns: Cleaning, Testing and Fault Isolation, Handling,
Packaging, Shipping, Troubleshooting.
Permitted as modifiers: air-conditioning system, degreasing agent, grinding
wheel, switching relay, welding torch.
The only approved dictionary words with an "-ing" form are: lighting, opening,
routing, servicing (nouns); mating, missing, remaining (adjectives); something
(pronoun); during (preposition).

**3.6 Use the active voice.** In descriptive writing, the passive voice is
permitted only when the agent is unknown.
Test: ask "by whom or by what?" If the text answers, it is passive.
Four fixes:
1. Put the agent first. "The circuits are connected by a switching relay" →
   "A switching relay connects the circuits."
2. Change an infinitive to an active verb. "These values are used by the
   computer to calculate X" → "The computer calculates X from these values."
3. In procedures, use the imperative. "The test can be continued by the
   operator" → "Continue the test."
4. Use "you" or "we" as the subject. "The valve can be opened with the override
   handle" → "You can open the valve with the override handle."
Permitted: "During transmission, the data was corrupted." (The agent is unknown.)

**3.7 Use an approved verb to describe an action, not a noun.**
"The ohmmeter gives an indication of 450 ohms" → "The ohmmeter shows 450 ohms."
"Before the removal of the unit" → "Before you remove the unit."
"Check the laptop battery" → "Do a check of the laptop battery." ("Check" is an
approved noun, not an approved verb.)

## Section 4 — Sentences

**4.1 Write short and clear sentences.**
In procedures, give short direct instructions in the imperative form.
In descriptive text, each sentence has one topic and no imperative form.
Do not write abstract sentences. "No leaks are permitted" → "Make sure that
there are no leaks." "Different temperatures will change the cure time" → "When
the temperature increases, the cure time will decrease."

**4.2 Do not omit words or use contractions.**
No "don't", "isn't", "it's". Do not omit nouns, verbs, subjects, or articles.
"Rotary switch to INPUT" → "Set the rotary switch to INPUT."
"If installed, remove the shims" → "If shims are installed, remove them."

**4.3 Use a vertical list for complex text.**
Colon before the list. Each item starts with an uppercase letter. Period at the
end of an item only when the item is a full sentence, and always after the last
item. No commas or semicolons at the ends of items. Use an article before the
subject noun of each item. Do not mix procedural and descriptive items in one
list. In safety lists, repeat "DO NOT" in each item.

**4.4 Use connecting words and phrases to connect related sentences.**
Approved connecting words: and, but, then, thus. Connecting phrases: "as a
result", "at the same time". Demonstrative adjectives also connect ideas.

**4.5 Use an article (the, a, an) or a demonstrative adjective (this, these)
before a noun or multi-word noun.**
"Turn shaft assembly" → "Turn the shaft assembly."
No article in general statements: "Solvents can cause damage to paint."
In a long series, the article before the first noun only: "Discard the O-rings
(3), gaskets (4), seals (7), and washers (9)."
No definite article before a noun with an alphanumeric identifier: "Tag circuit
breaker 36L7", not "the circuit breaker 36L7".

## Section 5 — Procedural writing

**5.1 Maximum 20 words per sentence.** Warnings and cautions obey this too.
Notes may go to 25 words (see 5.5).

**5.2 One instruction per sentence,** unless two actions occur at the same time.
Permitted together: "Cut and remove the wire." "Remove and discard the seal."
"Hold the panel in its open position and install the fastener."

**5.3 Write instructions in the imperative form.**
Do not use "must" before an imperative unless the instruction is a safety
instruction or an important condition.

**5.4 When a condition applies, write the condition first, then a comma, then
the command.**
"Set the switch to NORMAL when the light comes on" → "When the light comes on,
set the switch to NORMAL."

**5.5 Write notes to give information only, never instructions.**
A note must never contain the imperative form, and must never carry limits,
tolerances, or results — those belong in the work step. Test: read the procedure
without the notes. If the reader can still do it, the notes are correct.

## Section 6 — Descriptive writing

**6.1 Give information gradually.** One subject per sentence, built up in order.

**6.2 Use key words and key phrases to give the text a logical structure.**
Repeat the same key term rather than varying it. Sentence 2 picks up the key
words of sentence 1 and adds to them.

**6.3 Maximum 25 words per sentence.**

**6.4 Use paragraphs to show related information.** Start each paragraph with a
topic sentence.

**6.5 One topic per paragraph.** The topic sentences alone should read as a
usable outline of the document.

**6.6 Maximum six sentences per paragraph.**

## Section 7 — Safety instructions

**7.1 Use an applicable word to identify the level of risk.**
A warning: risk of injury or death. A caution: risk of damage to objects. If
both apply, use a warning. Do a real risk analysis before you choose.

**7.2 Start a safety instruction with a clear command or condition.**

**7.3 Give an explanation that shows the risk or possible result.**
"CAUTION: DO NOT USE BLEACH OR CLEANSERS THAT CONTAIN CHLORINE TO CLEAN THE
UNIT. THESE CLEANING AGENTS CAN CAUSE CORROSION."
STE gives no formatting rules. Uppercase in the standard's examples is a
convention of aerospace manuals, not an STE rule.

## Section 8 — Punctuation and word count

**8.1 All standard English punctuation is permitted except the semicolon (;).**
Write two sentences instead.

**8.2 Use hyphens to connect words that are directly related.**
low-altitude flight, quick-release fastener, forty-seven, L-shaped bracket,
heat-treat, de-icing, pre-amplifier.

**8.3 Parentheses are permitted** for references to illustrations or text, item
identifiers, work-step identifiers, abbreviations, singular/plural forms
("test(s)"), explanations, and alternatives.

**8.4 In a vertical list, a colon has the same effect on word count as a
period.** The lead-in and each item are counted separately.

**8.5 Text in parentheses counts as one word** in the host sentence, and also
forms a separate sentence of its own for counting.

**8.6 Each of these counts as one word:** numbers; a number with its unit
("10 °C" = 1 word); abbreviations (NASA, VPN, a.m.); alphanumeric identifiers
("No. 1", "36L7"); quoted text; titles, headings, placards and labels; proper
nouns of individuals, groups, organizations, and geopolitical entities.
Do not count numbers that identify paragraphs or work steps.

**8.7 Hyphenated words count as one word.**

## Section 9 — Writing practices

**9.1 Use a different sentence construction when a word-for-word replacement is
not sufficient.** Four situations force this: the grammar must change to fit the
alternative; the swap gives a meaningless result; the alternative changes the
meaning; or the word is not in the dictionary at all.
"Without this modification, the service life of the unit can be uncertain" →
"Without this modification, it is possible that the service life of this unit
will be shorter than usual."

**9.2 Use each approved word correctly.**
"Wear protective clothing" → "Put on protective clothing." (WEAR means "become
damaged by friction".)
"This regulation extends to all units" → "This regulation is applicable to all units."
"Move the tube to see if the connection is tight" → "... to make sure that the
connection is tight." (SEE is for the eyes only.)
"The indicator turns green" → "The color of the indicator changes to green."
"Do not let the pressure go below 20 psi" → "Do not let the pressure become less
than 20 psi." (ABOVE and BELOW are for physical positions, not limits.)
"When you work with cleaning agents" → "When you do work with cleaning agents."
(WORK is an approved noun, not a verb.)
"Be careful not to damage the sleeve" → "Be careful not to cause damage to the
sleeve." (DAMAGE is an approved noun, not a verb.)

**9.3 Do not make phrasal verbs.**
"After you put out the fire" → "After you extinguish the fire."
"This compound can give off poisonous fumes" → "This compound can release
poisonous fumes."
Only a few phrasal verbs are approved, with restricted meanings: "put on",
"come on".

**9.4 Use a consistent style.** Same wording for the same action, every time.

## General recommendations

**GR-1 The conjunction "that".** Keep it. "Make sure the valve is open" → "Make
sure that the valve is open."

**GR-2 The preposition "with".** It has three approved meanings and causes
ambiguity. Reread every sentence that uses it. "Use tool TS9867 to seal the
opening" → "Seal the opening with tool TS9867" (the primary action verb must be
"seal", not "use").

**GR-3 Pronouns.** Use only pronouns in the dictionary. If a pronoun could refer
to more than one noun, replace it with the noun.

**GR-4 The pronoun "this".** Make the referent explicit. "Make sure that the
cover is not locked (this can cause damage to the probe)" → "Make sure that the
cover is not locked. If the cover is locked, this can cause damage to the probe."

**GR-5 False friends.** Check that a word means in English what it means in your
first language.

**GR-6 Latin abbreviations.** Do not use e.g., i.e., etc. Write "for example",
"that is", "and so on".

**GR-7 Inclusive language.** Gender-specific pronouns ("he", "she") are not
permitted. "Man" and "woman" are not permitted unless the context requires them,
for example in a medical text.

**GR-8 Possessive form.** Permitted, but only when you are sure it is correct.
If you are not sure, rewrite without it.
